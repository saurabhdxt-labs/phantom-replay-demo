PHANTOM · ADJUDICATION

Did aircraft here report degraded GNSS integrity, and can you check it without taking my word for it?

A feed that settles that question for one hex cell and one UTC hour at a time, from a record committed before the hour began. This page walks one verdict end to end. Every figure is read out of this snapshot's own files; each one names its source file and field in its tooltip, and all of them are listed in the provenance manifest linked at the foot.

WHAT IT IS FOR

Settling a disputed hour after the fact — a delay, a diversion, a claim — with evidence your own customers can re-check independently. It is not a forecast and it does not attribute a cause.

1 · The verdict

DEGRADED
CELL 871fae670ffffff · 2026-10-10T13:00:00Z · EVIDENCE STRONG

DEGRADED is a reading of what was observed: the aircraft seen in that cell during that hour reported degraded navigation integrity often enough to cross a fixed threshold. The determination follows the observation and nothing else. The committed record plays a separate part: without a forecast committed in advance for that hour there is no verdict at all, only the refusal shown below. The evidence behind this one is graded STRONG, which is not a probability and not a confidence score — it describes how much was observed, nothing more.

2 · The receipt

The forecast covering this hour was committed at 2026-10-10T11:40:04Z as commit 60cf7d9c44ca0ae41a3b3172ea66f69f445f9af8, which is 79 minutes before the hour it describes began. That commit is anchored into Bitcoin block 970,761. The commit time is git’s own stamp, which the author sets; the block is the part you do not have to take on trust.

git clone https://github.com/saurabhdxt-labs/phantom-record-mirror
ots info proofs/mirror/60cf7d9c44ca0ae41a3b3172ea66f69f445f9af8.sha.ots

That command names a Bitcoin block. Look the block up on any public explorer and read its timestamp: it is an upper bound on when the commit, and every forecast inside it, already existed, to within Bitcoin’s usual timestamp tolerance of up to about two hours. Where that block was mined before the hour began, the forecast provably was not written in hindsight. Not every receipt in this snapshot clears that bar: for some hours the block landed after the hour had started, and the console shows the block on every verdict so you can check each one rather than take an average. What the ordering does not show is that the observations behind a determination are unaltered — those are not anchored, and this page does not claim they are.

WHAT THIS CHECK DOES NOT DO

Bitcoin attestation record present in the proof file; structural reading only, not verified against a Bitcoin node. A full ots verify against a Bitcoin node confirms the proof cryptographically; this snapshot reports only what the proof file asserts.

3 · What was excluded, and what was not

Three alternative explanations. A leg that was not assessed is reported as not assessed — it is never counted as satisfied, and an excluded leg rules an explanation out rather than ruling a cause in.

  • EXCLUDEDWhether enough distinct aircraft were observed in the cell — the floor for ruling out a single faulty receiver
  • NOT ASSESSEDWhether space weather was quiet — which would rule out a geomagnetic storm as an available explanation
  • NOT ESTABLISHEDWhether degradation began inside this window — rather than already running when the window opened

The verdict ships with its own limits:

  • Coverage limit: the adjudicability tier is STRONG; the observation count behind it is not disclosed.
  • no evidence is not the same as no event: this verdict reports only what the anchored record contains.
  • This is an evidence assembly from a pre-committed, anchored record; it makes no forecast.
  • Geomagnetic context unavailable for this window; this alternative explanation was not assessed.
  • Space weather was not assessed for this window, so it is not excluded as an explanation; this is not counted as satisfied.

4 · A refusal

The same cell at 2026-07-20T13:00:00Z, which is before this record begins on 2026-07-31. The observation is graded STRONG — the strongest tier — and the system still refuses, at the record stage:

DECLINED

no committed forecast row covers this area and hour

Strong evidence is not enough on its own. With nothing committed in advance for that hour there is nothing to adjudicate against, and the answer is a refusal rather than a late opinion. A system that declines is one you can believe when it does not.

5 · The shape of what we know

88CONSECUTIVE DAYS OF RECORD
1,919PROOF FILES WITH A BITCOIN ATTESTATION RECORD
63ONSET-CAPABLE DAYS, SEPARATE WINDOW
82%SPACE WEATHER UNASSESSED

The record runs 88 consecutive calendar days with 0 calendar-day gaps. That is a day-level count: a day enters the record once it holds a committed forecast, so it is not a claim about every hour inside those days. Its first two days were added a day late, after their hours had passed; live, hour-by-hour commitment begins on the third. Measured by the hour instead, 2,076 of 2,100 owed hours are present and 24 are absent, spread over 11 days — an hour is counted absent only once it has closed and had a two-hour settling grace. The console shows those hours as missing rather than averaging them away. Any cell with a committed row can be queried; the console lists, and this snapshot exports, only the cells the forecast flagged. The onset signal is thinner and separate: 63 capable days with 4 named gaps, so for many hours the question “did it start here?” cannot be answered at all. There are 1,782 per-commit proof files and 140 daily-head ones; of that total, 1,919 already carry a Bitcoin attestation and 3 are still waiting on a block.

Space weather could not be assessed for 2,828 of the 3,428 verdicts that were not refused (82%). That is a limit of this snapshot, not a finding about space weather, and those cells are not painted quiet.

READ THE DENOMINATOR BEFORE THE PERCENTAGES

This snapshot is a deliberately selected sample, not a census. The export is configured to take, from each of the 70 hours, up to 60 cells ranked by evidence plus 3 unobserved ones; in sparse hours that top slice is itself partly unobserved. That gives 4,410 verdicts, drawn from the 49,339 cell-hours the forecast flagged at or above its probability threshold in this window — not from every cell-hour in it, which is a far larger number. The 2 pinned examples bring the total to 4,412, of which 984 are refusals. Because the sample is ranked by evidence, none of these proportions is the system's rate over all cells and none should be read as one.
